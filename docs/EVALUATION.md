# Evaluation of the risk indicators

Generated by `veedor evaluate report` from `eval/backtest.json` (run 2026-11-16 07:48 UTC, seed 20260101, 2,000 bootstrap replicates) and `eval/coverage.json`. Do not edit by hand: every number below is read from those files.

Veedor reports risk indicators, never accusations. This document asks a narrower question: do the indicators carry any measurable signal against the only external label available?

## Summary for non-specialists

**What was tested.** Veedor gives each public contract a risk score from simple rules (for example, a single bidder). The only official outside list we could use is the SRI's published list of "empresas fantasmas" (shell companies): 1,113 tax numbers, of which 9 are companies that won public contracts. We asked whether the score ranks those companies higher than the other 19,837 contract-winning companies, after removing the rules that use the list itself.

**Result.** Chance level is an AUC of 0.5; the score reached 0.447 (0.303 to 0.598), and the interval includes 0.5. Keeping the time-aware inactive-supplier rule gives 0.447 (0.303 to 0.598), which includes 0.5. The available evidence does not show that the score singles out the listed suppliers. Across 4 runs after rule changes the headline AUC went from 0.474 to 0.447; the conclusion did not change.

**What it means in practice.** With so few listed companies the test cannot rule out a modest real effect, and being on the list is not the same as a problematic contract. The indicators should be read as descriptive transparency measures that point to atypical features of a process for a human to look at. They are not validated predictors of wrongdoing, and a high score is not an accusation.

## What this does and does not show

**It shows** how a score built from the indicators ranks suppliers that the SRI later published as "empresas fantasmas" relative to all other awarded companies, once the signals that encode the label are removed, and how wide the uncertainty is with so few positives.

**It does not show** that any indicator detects improper procurement. The label is a tax-administration classification of a company, not a finding about a contract. A score that fails to separate these companies says little about whether it flags atypical processes in general, and a score that succeeds would not prove wrongdoing either. There is no labelled set of proven problem contracts in the open data, so predictive validity for the purpose the indicators serve (prioritising processes for human review) is **not established** by this evaluation.

## Method

### Data and label

- Processes scored: 2,251,027. Signals are read from `process_signals`; the check that rebuilt flags equal the `signal_codes` list of `process_scores` found 0 mismatching processes.
- Label: the SRI list of "empresas fantasmas" (1,113 RUCs). 16 of them won at least one process as the process winner (the supplier with the largest award in `process_scores`): 9 companies (44 processes) and 7 natural persons (121 processes).
- Positive-unlabelled setting: a supplier that is not listed is **unlabelled**, not known to be clean. Some unlabelled suppliers may resemble the listed ones, which pushes every metric toward chance.
- **Primary universe: companies only** (RUC third digit 9): 9 positives against 19,837 unlabelled companies. Natural persons and companies differ in which signals can fire at all (for example the company-size signal needs Superintendencia financials), so mixing them would make the comparison unlike-for-like. A sensitivity analysis with both kinds is reported separately.

### Leakage control

`GHOST_COMPANY_LIST` is the label and is always removed. `SUPPLIER_NOT_ACTIVE` is time-aware in the current marts (it fires only when the suspension came before the process date), but it is still derived from SRI status, which can be a consequence of being listed. The headline variant A removes it as a precaution; variant C keeps it so the effect of that choice is visible. The score is recomputed from `process_signals` with the production weights (sum of the weights of distinct fired signals, capped at 100):

- **A**: held-out score A: without both ghost-list codes and SUPPLIER_NOT_ACTIVE (excluded: `GHOST_COMPANY_LIST`, `GHOST_COMPANY_LISTED_LATER`, `SUPPLIER_NOT_ACTIVE`)
- **B**: held-out score B: A and also without CONTRACT_VS_COMPANY_SIZE (excluded: `CONTRACT_VS_COMPANY_SIZE`, `GHOST_COMPANY_LIST`, `GHOST_COMPANY_LISTED_LATER`, `SUPPLIER_NOT_ACTIVE`)
- **C**: score C: keeps the time-aware SUPPLIER_NOT_ACTIVE, excludes only the ghost-list codes (excluded: `GHOST_COMPANY_LIST`, `GHOST_COMPANY_LISTED_LATER`)

Evidence on whether it could still be affected by the listing (a check, not a proof; the same-year variant is a context signal and is not scored):

7 processes of positive companies fired `CONTRACT_VS_COMPANY_SIZE`; 0 of them were awarded on or after the notification date, and 0 used a revenue year at or after the notification year. The signal needs a positive revenue row in the Superintendencia data: 44.4% of positive companies (9) have one, against 73.4% of unlabelled companies (19,837).

Evidence on `SUPPLIER_NOT_ACTIVE` (can it still leak?):

The signal fires 16 times in total; 0 of those firings are on processes won by listed suppliers (0 on companies), and 0 of them are on awards dated on or after the SRI notification date. Because it fires on no positive process, variant C is numerically identical to variant A for the positives and the status signal cannot be leaking the label here.

All variants are reported. Variant A is the headline.

### Supplier-level back-test

Each supplier's held-out process scores are aggregated three ways: mean, max and the share of its processes with at least one signal. Suppliers are ranked by each aggregate. Metrics: ROC-AUC and average precision (ties are handled as blocks; ties at a cut-off are split proportionally), and lift in the top 1%, 5% and 10% (precision in the top fraction divided by the base rate). Confidence intervals are percentile intervals from a supplier-level bootstrap, stratified by label so that every replicate keeps the same number of positives. Each replicate re-draws suppliers, so the dependence between the processes of one supplier is respected. The permutation p-value is the share of 5,000 random labelings with the same number of positives whose AUC is at least the observed one.

The max and share aggregates favour suppliers with many processes; the baseline "number of processes won" is reported to make that visible. The aggregates take few distinct values, so large blocks of suppliers are tied (most at zero) and lift at different cut-offs can coincide. A random ranking has average precision equal to the prevalence.

### Temporal honesty

Awards made after the SRI notification date are not predictive evidence (the signals could already reflect the listing). The temporal analysis keeps only each positive's processes dated before its notification date, and drops positives with no notification date. The award date is `first_award_date`, falling back to `date_signed`, then `release_date`. Unlabelled suppliers are not restricted.

### Per-signal view, baselines, robustness

Per signal: the rate of firing among the processes of positives against the processes of unlabelled suppliers, their ratio, and intervals from the same supplier-level bootstrap. Baselines: random ranking (AUC 0.5, lift 1, average precision equal to prevalence), total amount awarded, and number of processes won. Robustness: each positive process is compared with unlabelled company-won processes of the same method (all catalogue conventions are one stratum) and the same year, giving the stratified concordance (probability that a positive process outscores a same-stratum unlabelled one, ties counting half; 0.5 means no difference) and observed versus expected mean score and any-signal share. Only positives are resampled there; the unlabelled strata are held fixed.

## Limitations

- **Positive-unlabelled labels.** Unlisted suppliers are unlabelled, not clean; contamination biases every metric toward chance.
- **Very few positives.** 9 positive companies. The intervals are wide and a null result cannot be separated from a weak real effect. Metrics such as lift in the top 1% rest on a handful of suppliers at most.
- **The label is not procurement wrongdoing.** The SRI classification concerns tax-reporting behaviour of a company. It may correlate with contract risk or not.
- **Uncalibrated weights.** The weights are judgement calls; nothing here tunes them, and a different weighting could rank differently.
- **Survivorship.** Only listed suppliers that won a process in the open data can be positives. Suppliers that were listed before ever winning, or whose awards are missing, are invisible.
- **Current-status leakage.** `SUPPLIER_NOT_ACTIVE` uses the status in a 2026 file. It is excluded here, but other registry-derived fields may carry similar hindsight.
- **Contract-size signal.** The check above is limited to what the data can show; the variant B result is reported so the reader can see whether the conclusion depends on it.
- **Emergency procurement is missing** from the open data, and it is where much documented concern lies. Indicators are untested there.
- **Aggregation choices.** Supplier-level aggregates favour suppliers with many processes (max, share) and can hide single flagged processes (mean); none of them is the unit analysts review.
- **Winner attribution.** A process's winner is the supplier with the largest award; co-awardees are not evaluated.

signal for "which signals", both relation facts for the history). (2) The decision is taken over all intents, including an `oos` intent (what the

answered with a neighbouring fact. (3) A named signal is matched by the distinctive words of its label. (4) Eight numbers (floor, k, mix weight,
embedding rescaling, margins) were chosen by coordinate descent on the tuning half of the processes with the original and the development phrasings;
the held-out set was not used. (5) Screen: company-name questions and a few colloquial judgement and instruction wordings (favouritism, "sin
restricciones", requests for a poem) were added; nothing was relaxed for questions about persons.
